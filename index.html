Projected housekeeping suggestion

Estimate, not a quote. The suggested 2.5% of purchase price is a conservative allowance. The operator's estimate is $145 per turnover (varies by room type) plus $20 per occupied night. At 63% occupancy (HVS), a suite is occupied about 229.95 nights a year. A 3-night average stay gives about 77 turnovers, about $15,713 a year; a 2-night average gives about 115 turnovers, about $21,270 a year. On a $1,091,675 two-bedroom, 2.5% is about $27,292, roughly $6,022 above the 2-night estimate, which lowers projected owner profit by that amount. Stay-length benchmarks: SiteMinder's U.S. summer 2026 booking sample, 2.22 nights (reported by Hotel Management); Cloudbeds' international independent-hotel benchmark, 2.6 nights; Pinellas County January hotel samples, 2.5 days (2024), 2.8 (2025) and 2.3 (2026). These support testing 2–3-night stays; they do not establish this property's actual average.

How the Figures Are Built

  • Room revenue = ADR × occupancy × 365 nights. The 2030 ADR is yours to set (HVS suggestion shown). Each later year grows 7% a year, the HVS study's annual rate, and is not editable. Suggested occupancy follows HVS Market Research (February 1, 2025), held at 75% in years 4–5.
  • Unit list shows only available units (green on the inventory grid printed October 10, 2026), sorted by price. The suggested purchase price is that unit's list price; enter the actual contract price.
  • Cash flow before taxes (a) = net operating income − mortgage payment. Total annual return adds principal paid down (b) and appreciation (c), measured against cash invested.
  • Terminal value = net operating income ÷ cap rate (8% suggested for a condo-hotel suite). Multiple of invested capital = (terminal value − remaining loan + cumulative cash flow) ÷ cash invested. Selling costs are not deducted.
  • Suggested growth figures: 4.0% appreciation reflects a flatter downtown Tampa condo market than the 2021–22 run-up, with new luxury product holding value better than resale. 3.0% inflation sits above the Fed's 2% target to allow for Florida insurance and labor costs.
  • Not included: owner stays, income taxes, depreciation or cost segregation, resort fees, closing and selling costs.
